# Review what the reader is trying to do

Book basis: Chapter 3, pages 16-27, and Chapter 6, pages 38-45. Use all seven Checklist 1 items in [the review worksheet](book-checklist.md). The brief and evidence fields below are review methods for applying those items, not extra book requirements.

## Build an intent brief

Read the title, main text, page type, audience cues, navigation context, and next action. A keyword is a topic clue. It is not an intent by itself.

Record these fields for each selected page:

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Audience and situation | Who is reading and what constraint brought them here? |
| Primary intent | Learn, compare or choose, buy or act, navigate, or solve a problem. State the concrete task. |
| Evidence | Quote short passages and give source sections or lines. |
| Supporting questions | What, why, how, requirements, cost, alternatives, limitations, or troubleshooting, as relevant. |
| Coverage | Answered, partial, missing, or outside this page's purpose, with evidence for each. |
| Next action | The step that helps the reader complete the task. |
| Confidence | Explain whether intent is explicit, inferred, or unresolved. |

For example, a cloud storage pricing page serves a plan-selection task. Storage limits, team size, sharing needs, billing terms, and migration constraints can support that task. A glossary definition of cloud storage cannot replace those answers.

## Assess the actual answers

Make sure that the title signals the task (C1.2) and that the text answers it (C1.1). Inspect the opening as evidence; the book sets no mandatory answer position. Inspect whether headings follow the reader's decisions or steps. Keep related terminology and named entities consistent. Explain unfamiliar terms when the audience needs them.

Use specific situations and natural questions where they fit. Address predictable follow-up questions without diluting the page's purpose. A comparison benefits from explicit criteria and tradeoffs. A tutorial benefits from prerequisites, steps, expected results, and recovery from common failures.

Distinguish a useful short answer from an unsupported claim. Chapter 8, page 77 emphasizes human expertise and accuracy. Keep sources, authorship, examples, and dates where they support that review. Do not manufacture expertise, research, numerical claims, or customer evidence.

## Edit and reassess

Preserve the original language and voice. Add useful material from supplied facts or verified sources. If a material business fact is missing, ask for it or mark the gap. Do not fill it with a plausible invention.

The book recommends both a general FAQ page and embedded FAQs (pages 42-44). Inspect existing coverage of each, then add a FAQ where recurring questions remain useful after improving the main text. Explicitly record why a FAQ does not fit if that is the decision. Keep FAQ answers visible and synchronized with any structured data. Do not force a question into every heading or add a FAQ to every page.

After editing, repeat the intent brief against the rendered result. Report the gaps resolved and those still open. Do not use keyword density, arbitrary heading counts, or a numeric intent score as a substitute for this reasoning.
